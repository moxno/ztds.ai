SUBJECT: Legal & Architectural Basis for Zero-Trust Data Sanitization (ZTDS) & Exemption from Data Processing Agreements (DPA)

1. Executive Summary

This memorandum establishes the formal technical and regulatory basis under which software conforming to the Zero-Trust Data Sanitization (ZTDS v1.0) specification operates as a 100% client-side computational utility.

Because conforming software executes de-identification and re-identification strictly within volatile client RAM before outbound network transmission, exactly zero (0) bytes of cleartext sensitive data are transmitted to or processed by external vendor servers.

Consequently, ZTDS-conforming architectures are legally exempt from third-party Data Processing Agreement (DPA) requirements under GDPR Article 28, Business Associate Agreement (BAA) burdens under HIPAA, and SaaS sub-processor risk exposure.

2. Statutory Grounds for Exemption

A. European Union GDPR (Regulation 2016/679) — Article 28 Exemption

Statutory Requirement: Article 28(3) mandates a binding contract (DPA) whenever personal data is processed by a third party on behalf of the controller.
Exemption Doctrine: In a ZTDS architecture, the software maintainer operates zero ingestion endpoints, maintains zero persistent databases, and handles zero customer traffic. All transformations occur exclusively inside the customer's private workstation memory or private VPC node. Under European Data Protection Board (EDPB) doctrine, providing localized client-side computational code that never transmits personal data to the vendor does not constitute "processing by a data processor."
Conclusion: No GDPR DPA or Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) are required with the ZTDS software maintainer.

B. GDPR Article 17 — Prevention of RAG Vector Database Poisoning

Statutory Requirement: Article 17 grants data subjects an unconditional Right to Erasure ("Right to be Forgotten").
Exemption Doctrine: When cleartext PII is embedded into high-dimensional vector representations, individual personal data cannot be excised without full index re-computation. By replacing sensitive entities with surrogate tokens ([NAME_1], [IBAN_1]) prior to vector embedding generation, vector databases are mathematically immunized against PII ingestion, ensuring permanent compliance with GDPR Article 17 without data re-indexing.

C. United States HIPAA (45 CFR § 164.514) — Safe Harbor Standard

Statutory Requirement: 45 CFR § 164.502(e) mandates a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) when Protected Health Information (PHI) is disclosed to a third party.
Exemption Doctrine: Under the Safe Harbor method (45 CFR § 164.514(b)), health information stripped of the 18 designated personal identifiers is legally classified as de-identified and ceases to constitute PHI. Because stripping occurs locally before transmission, cloud LLM endpoints receive only de-identified surrogate tokens.
Conclusion: No BAA is legally required with the local sanitization engine or cloud AI endpoints for sanitized payloads.

D. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — Control A.8.11 (Data Masking)

ZTDS directly satisfies Control A.8.11 ("Data masking shall be applied in accordance with the organization's topic-specific policy on access control...") by enforcing deterministic, contextual pseudonymization at the presentation and API layer.

4. Five-Step Airplane Mode Audit Protocol

Enterprise security teams can independently reproduce and verify the zero-egress invariant in under 60 seconds using standard developer tools:

  1. Open browser Developer Tools (F12 or Cmd+Opt+I) and navigate to the Network tab.
  2. Load the sanitization interface (ztds.ai/inspector/ or PrivacyScrubber extension).
  3. Disconnect network connection or enable physical Airplane Mode.
  4. Paste confidential text containing PII (names, credit cards, emails) and execute the sanitization transformation.
  5. Confirm that surrogate tokens are generated instantly and zero network requests are initiated or queued.
