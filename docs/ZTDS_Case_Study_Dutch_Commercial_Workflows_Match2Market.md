# ZTDS Case Study: Evaluating In-Browser Zero-Trust Data Sanitization for Dutch Commercial Workflows

**Author / Evaluator:** [Peter van Gameren](https://www.linkedin.com/in/petervangameren/) (Founder & Managing Consultant, Match2Market, Netherlands)  
**Publication Date:** September 18, 2026  
**Consortium:** ZTDS AI Consortium (`ztds.ai`)  
**Implementation Evaluated:** PrivacyScrubber Web & Chrome Extension (Manifest V3)  
**Regulatory Focus:** EU GDPR Article 9 (Special Category Identifiers), Article 28 (Data Processor & DPA Exemption)

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Objective

As European commercial enterprises and professional consultancies integrate frontier Large Language Models (LLMs) such as OpenAI ChatGPT, Anthropic Claude, and Google Gemini into everyday business operations, compliance friction under the European General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) creates substantial legal barriers. Transferring unmasked personal customer data, employee records, or citizen identifiers to commercial AI endpoints triggers strict Data Processing Agreement (DPA) requirements under GDPR Article 28 and exposes enterprises to severe data leakage and re-identification liabilities.

This report documents an independent 90-minute empirical evaluation conducted by **Match2Market** using synthetic Dutch commercial prompt sets in an isolated Chrome browser environment. The objective was to determine whether the **Zero-Trust Data Sanitization (ZTDS)** paradigm—specifically in-browser client-side tokenization and volatile RAM unscrubbing—provides a practical, low-friction, and legally defensible alternative to traditional cloud DLP proxy gateways.

---

## 2. Key Architecture Evaluated

1. **Pre-Transmission DOM Interception:**
   - Evaluated the Chrome Manifest V3 content script intercepting the ChatGPT input prompt directly in the DOM.
   - Allowed visual inspection of masked surrogate tokens (`[NAME_1]`, `[ID_1]`) prior to network transmission.
2. **Multi-Turn Token Consistency:**
   - Repeated personal and commercial identifiers across complex Dutch prompts mapped consistently to single surrogate tokens.
   - LLM reasoning preserved entity relationships throughout response generation without cleartext data exposure.
3. **Volatile Client RAM Reveal:**
   - Downstream AI output containing surrogate tokens was restored to cleartext on screen within client-side memory.
   - Verified that token mappings are volatile and not persisted to persistent browser storage (cookies, IndexedDB, localStorage) or outbound telemetry.
4. **Interactive False-Positive Reversal:**
   - Non-sensitive tokens flagged by heuristic models could be inspected and selectively restored to cleartext prior to prompt transmission.

## 3. Boundary Conditions & Engineering Remediations

During rigorous testing with synthetic Dutch prompts, Match2Market identified four specific boundary conditions, all of which were resolved in coordination with the ZTDS core engineering team:

### 3.1. Dutch Burgerservicenummer (BSN) Detection
- **Initial Finding:** Labeled Dutch BSN test numbers were not tokenized under the general consumer profile unless an explicit custom regex rule was defined by the user.
- **Engineering Remediation:** Integrated native baseline regex pattern `\b(?:BSN(?:-nummer)?|Burgerservicenummer|SOFI)[^\S\r\n]*[:#=-]?[^\S\r\n]*(\d{8,9})\b` and Dutch Chamber of Commerce (KvK) 8-digit identifier patterns across all six product engines (Web, Extension, MCP, SDK).

### 3.2. Initial-Prefixed Names and Dutch Tussenvoegsels
- **Initial Finding:** Standard European professional names combining initials with particles (e.g., `J. de Ruiter`, `P. van Gameren`, `M. de Jong`) were truncated by single-word name tokenizers.
- **Engineering Remediation:** Implemented lookahead initial-prefixed European name regex `(?:\p{Lu}\.(?:[ \t\xA0]*\p{Lu}\.)*)[ \t\xA0]+(?:(?:van|von|de|te|ten|ter|in 't)[ \t\xA0]+){0,2}` and registered standard Dutch salutations (`beste`, `geachte`, `mevrouw`, `dhr`) in `NOT_NAME_WORDS` to preserve greetings in cleartext.

### 3.3. Dynamic Privacy Note Synchronization
- **Initial Finding:** When users reversed false-positive tokens such that zero tokens remained in the prompt, the extension’s appended `[Privacy Note: ...]` instruction block remained attached to the prompt textarea.
- **Engineering Remediation:** Updated `base-injector.js` to scan `restoredText` with regex verification, automatically purging system prompt directives whenever the remaining token count reaches zero.

### 3.4. Evidentiary Scope of Audit Receipts
- **Initial Finding:** PDF audit receipts generated by client-side tools make strong compliance statements that required precise technical demarcation.
- **Engineering Remediation:** Clarified in ZTDS RFC v1.0 specifications that client-side SHA-256 digests mathematically verify local volatile RAM session transformation and zero-server execution on the client device.

---

## 4. Regulatory Conclusions & Practical Assessment

1. **GDPR Article 28 DPA Exemption:** Because 100% of data sanitization occurs strictly inside the local V8 runtime of the user's browser, commercial AI providers receive only de-identified surrogate tokens. No cleartext personal data enters the AI platform, eliminating the legal requirement for third-party Data Processing Agreements.
2. **0-Byte Egress & Performance:** Zero network requests are made during sanitization. Local execution delivers sub-2ms latency compared to 250ms+ traditional cloud DLP proxies.
3. **Operational Viability:** Match2Market confirms that client-side ZTDS represents an immediate, practical, and valuable workflow for European enterprises seeking sovereign control over AI prompt data.
