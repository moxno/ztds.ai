EU AI ACT · ARTICLE 10 · DATA GOVERNANCE

EU AI Act Article 10 Data Governance: What Sanitization Contributes

Article 10 of the EU AI Act sets data and data governance requirements for high-risk AI systems that use training, validation, and testing datasets. Sanitizing personal data before it enters a dataset or a model pipeline is one practical way to support those practices. It does not replace the provider obligations.

Summary

Article 10 requires providers of high-risk AI systems to apply appropriate data governance and management practices to the datasets they use, including attention to data collection processes and the origin of data, data-preparation operations such as annotation, labelling, cleaning, and aggregation, an assessment of the availability, quantity, and suitability of the data sets, and examination for possible biases. These duties apply to training, validation, and testing data sets where a high-risk system is developed using techniques that train AI models; for systems that do not train models, they apply to the testing data sets.

Where datasets are assembled from operational records, removing direct identifiers before the data reaches annotation tools, fine-tuning jobs, or external model services reduces the personal data in scope. ZTDS provides that step as a deterministic, reversible, client-side operation.

It is not legal advice, not a certification by the framework owner, and not a statement that any regulation is fully satisfied. Consult qualified counsel for jurisdiction-specific determinations.

How ZTDS Applies

Use the four invariants as the sanitization contract for dataset work:

  • Invariant 1 — Zero cleartext egress: identifiers are replaced before any network socket call leaves the host boundary.
  • Invariant 2 — Deterministic reversible surrogates: tokens such as [EMAIL_1] preserve syntax and meaning so the model remains useful, and the mapping is restored locally.
  • Invariant 3 — Volatile RAM isolation: the token map lives in host memory only and is zeroized at the end of the session.
  • Invariant 4 — Zero external subprocessors: no third party participates in the sanitization step.

Document the sanitization profile used for each dataset version so reviewers can see which entity categories were removed.

What This Control Does Not Cover

  • Article 10 duties rest on the provider of the high-risk system. A tool does not transfer or discharge them.
  • Sanitization does not address representativeness, bias examination, or error assessment of datasets.
  • Article 10(5) allows providers to exceptionally process special categories of personal data where strictly necessary for bias detection and correction, only if the purpose cannot be met with synthetic or anonymised data and subject to safeguards that include pseudonymisation and a ban on transmission to other parties. Removing identifiers can conflict with that purpose, so choose the sanitization profile per dataset.
  • Whether a given system is high-risk is a legal classification that requires its own analysis.
  • This page is not a conformity assessment and carries no authority from EU institutions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does EU AI Act Article 10 require removing personal data from training sets?

Article 10 requires appropriate data governance and management practices for datasets used by high-risk AI systems. Minimizing personal data is one practice that supports it, but the Article does not prescribe a single technique and the obligation stays with the provider.

Can sanitization make a system compliant with the EU AI Act?

No. Sanitization supports one area, data handling. Compliance requires a full assessment of the system classification and every applicable obligation.

Why use reversible tokens instead of deleting identifiers?

Reversible surrogates keep text coherent for annotation and model use, and the controller can restore original values locally when a legitimate need arises, while the external party never holds them.
