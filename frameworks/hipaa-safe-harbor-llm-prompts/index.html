HIPAA · 45 CFR 164.514(b)(2) · SAFE HARBOR

HIPAA Safe Harbor for LLM Prompts: Removing the 18 Identifiers Before Egress

HIPAA Safe Harbor (45 CFR 164.514(b)(2)) de-identifies clinical data by removing 18 identifier categories when no re-identification knowledge exists. Client-side tokenization prepares notes before model egress.

Summary

Safe Harbor is a rule-based method. It lists identifier categories of the individual and of relatives, employers, and household members that must be removed, including names, geographic subdivisions smaller than a state, date elements other than year, contact numbers, and record numbers.

Section 164.514(c) lets a covered entity assign a re-identification code unrelated to patient attributes, kept unshared with any external party.

Ephemeral session surrogates mapped strictly in volatile client memory satisfy that constraint, whereas deterministic hashes derived from patient values do not.

Scope notice. This page maps a technical control to a published framework as a Statutory Risk Minimization and Defense-in-Depth measure. It is not legal advice, not a certification by the framework owner, and not a statement that any regulation is fully satisfied. Consult qualified counsel for jurisdiction-specific determinations.

How ZTDS Applies

To keep the re-identification code consistent with Section 164.514(c):

  • Generate surrogates as session counters, not as hashes or transforms of the original values.
  • Keep the mapping on the covered entity side in volatile memory and never transmit it.
  • Zeroize the mapping when the session ends unless the covered entity has a documented reason to retain it.
  • Retain a local receipt of entity categories and counts as evidence of the step.

What This Control Does Not Cover

  • Safe Harbor requires no actual knowledge that the remaining information could identify an individual. A tool cannot establish that on its own.
  • Clinical narratives can contain details that identify a person outside the 18 categories; expert determination is the alternative method for those cases.
  • This page is not a legal opinion and does not constitute a determination by HHS.
  • Business associate and contractual questions remain separate from the de-identification method.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the HIPAA Safe Harbor method require?

Under 45 CFR 164.514(b)(2), the covered entity removes 18 listed categories of identifiers and has no actual knowledge that the remaining information could identify an individual. If both hold, the information is treated as de-identified.

Can reversible surrogates be used under Safe Harbor?

A code that permits re-identification is allowed under 164.514(c) if it is not derived from or related to information about the individual, is not otherwise capable of being translated to identify the individual, the code is not used or disclosed for other purposes, and the covered entity does not disclose the mechanism for re-identification. Random session surrogates whose mapping stays only on the covered entity side meet that design constraint.

Is Safe Harbor enough for free-text clinical notes?

Not always. Free text can carry identifying details beyond the listed categories. Where residual risk remains, the expert determination method or additional review is the appropriate route.
