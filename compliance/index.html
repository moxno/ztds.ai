8 Global Regulatory Frameworks & Safe Harbor Mechanics

EU Regulation 2016/679 · Art. 28 Invariant 1 & Invariant 4

1. GDPR Article 28 — Subprocessor Scope Reduction & Zero-DPA Architecture

Article 28 mandates binding Data Processing Addenda (DPAs) whenever a third party processes personal data on behalf of a Controller. Because a ZTDS-compliant appliance executes 100% locally within the Controller's execution perimeter with zero telemetry and zero network egress, the sanitization software vendor never touches personal data under Article 4(2)—eliminating vendor subprocessor onboarding friction.

Procurement Impact: Zero Vendor DPA Required
EU Regulation 2016/679 · Art. 32(1)(a) Invariant 2 & Invariant 3

2. GDPR Article 32 — Security of Processing & Technical Safe Harbor

Article 32(1)(a) explicitly designates pseudonymization as a primary technical measure to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk. Rather than claiming an unenforceable blanket exemption for arbitrary free-text prompts, ZTDS establishes Defense-in-Depth Technical Safe Harbor: 100% direct PII blocking in RAM, contextual entropy reduction via domain profiles, and cryptographic SHA-256 audit receipts proving executive due diligence.

Liability Posture: Statutory Risk Minimization
EU Regulation 2016/679 · Recital 26 & Art. 4(5) Invariant 2

3. GDPR Recital 26 — Client-Bounded Pseudonymization vs. Downstream AI Providers

Under Recital 26 and European Court of Justice jurisprudence (SRB v. EDPS, Case T-557/20), whether tokenized payload constitutes personal data in the hands of a recipient depends on whether the recipient possesses legal and practical means likely reasonably to be used to re-identify the subject. Because the ZTDS mapping table never leaves client volatile RAM, the downstream LLM provider receives only unkeyed syntactic surrogates (`[PERSON_TOKEN_1]`).

Jurisprudence Alignment: CJEU T-557/20 Aligned
US HHS · 45 CFR § 164.514(b)(2) & (c) Invariant 1 & Invariant 2

4. HIPAA Safe Harbor — 18-Identifier De-Identification & Re-Identification Code Isolation

Under 45 CFR § 164.514(b)(2), Protected Health Information (PHI) is de-identified when all 18 specified identifier classes (names, MRNs, SSNs, dates, contact details, device serials) are stripped prior to leaving the Covered Entity's boundary. Under § 164.514(c), a Covered Entity may assign a non-derived re-identification code (`[PATIENT_TOKEN_1]`) provided the translation key is never disclosed outside the Covered Entity.

Clinical Boundary: 45 CFR § 164.514(b)-(c)
EU Regulation 2024/1689 · Art. 10 & Art. 15 Invariant 1 & Invariant 3

5. EU AI Act Articles 10 & 15 — Data Governance, RAG Hygiene & Cybersecurity Resilience

The EU AI Act requires deployers and providers of high-risk and general-purpose AI integrations to enforce strict data governance (Article 10) and robustness against data poisoning or prompt-extraction attacks (Article 15). By sanitizing documents prior to vector embedding (`Pinecone`/`Qdrant`) and tool invocation (`MCP`), ZTDS physically prevents cross-tenant PII regurgitation and vector-store inversion leaks.

AI Governance Scope: Pre-Embedding & Pre-Prompt
US Federal Rules of Evidence · Rule 502(b) & ABA Rule 1.6(c) Invariant 1 & Invariant 2

6. US FRE Rule 502(b) & ABA Model Rule 1.6(c) — Attorney-Client Privilege Preservation

Submitting unredacted M&A agreements, litigation briefs, or board minutes to external commercial AI models risks third-party privilege waiver under FRE Rule 502(b) and breaches of reasonable confidentiality efforts under ABA Formal Opinion 512. ZTDS strips party identities, docket references, and deal valuation figures in local memory before external LLM reasoning, preserving privilege over the underlying legal analysis.

Evidentiary Shield: Reasonable Technical Steps
AICPA TSC CC6.1 / CC6.6 · ISO/IEC 27701:2019 Invariant 3 & Invariant 4

7. SOC 2 Type II (CC6.1 / CC6.6) & ISO/IEC 27701 — Boundary Transmission Controls

SOC 2 Common Criteria CC6.6 requires logical boundary protection against unauthorized transmission of confidential data outside the system boundary, while ISO/IEC 27701 Clause 7.4 governs privacy by design and data minimization. Integrating `npx ztds-audit` into CI/CD pipelines (`ztds-audit.yml`) produces deterministic SHA-256 artifacts for external auditors verifying continuous boundary enforcement.

Audit Artifact: SHA-256 CI/CD Receipt
Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(m) & § 1798.140(v) Invariant 1 & Invariant 4

8. CCPA / CPRA — De-Identified Information Exclusion & Third-Party Sharing Prevention

Under California Civil Code § 1798.140(v)(2), "Personal Information" explicitly excludes de-identified consumer information where the business implements technical safeguards that prohibit re-identification by downstream recipients. Pre-egress ZTDS tokenization ensures that external AI API calls do not constitute unauthorized third-party sharing or disclosure of California consumer records.

Statutory Exclusion: Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(m)

SIG Lite: K.3.1 · CSA CAIQ: CEK-03 · VSAQ: Key Management & Zeroization STATUS: COMPLIANT (VOLATILE RAM ONLY)

How are cryptographic keys and re-identification token mapping tables stored, isolated, and destroyed?

Re-identification token maps (`tokenMap`) are instantiated exclusively in ephemeral client volatile memory (RAM) scoped to the active execution session. They are never written to disk, `localStorage`, `IndexedDB`, or remote databases, and are deterministically zeroized upon session termination (`resetSession()`). Normative Legal & Audit Q&A

Frequently Asked Statutory & DPO Questions

Does a ZTDS-compliant client-side sanitization appliance require a Data Processing Addendum (DPA) under GDPR Article 28? +

No Data Processing Addendum (DPA) is required with the sanitization vendor when running a certified ZTDS Level 1–3 client-side or air-gapped appliance. Because tokenization executes 100% within the data controller's volatile memory (RAM) with zero external network egress and zero third-party subprocessors (Invariant 4), the software vendor never receives, stores, or processes personal data under GDPR Article 4(2) and Article 28.

How does ZTDS provide Technical Safe Harbor under GDPR Article 32 without claiming absolute exemption for free-text prompts? +

ZTDS operates under a strict Statutory Risk Minimization and Defense-in-Depth model. While free-text narrative prompts can theoretically contain contextual quasi-identifiers, ZTDS establishes GDPR Article 32 Technical Safe Harbor through three verifiable layers: (1) 100% Direct PII Zero-Egress via client RAM tokenization before any network socket call, (2) Contextual Entropy Reduction using specialized industry profiles, and (3) Cryptographic SHA-256 Compliance Receipts that provide Data Protection Officers (DPOs) and CISOs with auditable proof of state-of-the-art technical due diligence.

How does ZTDS satisfy HIPAA Safe Harbor De-Identification under 45 CFR § 164.514(b)(2) for clinical AI workflows? +

Under HIPAA 45 CFR § 164.514(b)(2), health information is de-identified when all 18 specified direct and temporal identifiers are removed prior to external transmission, and the covered entity has no actual knowledge that the residual information can identify an individual. ZTDS clinical profiles intercept and replace all 18 identifier categories with deterministic syntactic tokens inside the covered entity's clinical perimeter before invoking external LLMs, keeping the private re-identification key strictly on-premise under 45 CFR § 164.514(c).

How does pre-egress tokenization prevent inadvertent waiver of attorney-client privilege under US Federal Rules of Evidence Rule 502(b)? +

Under US Federal Rules of Evidence Rule 502(b), a disclosure does not operate as a privilege waiver if the holder took reasonable steps to prevent disclosure of privileged identifiers. Deploying deterministic client-side tokenization to strip client names, matter numbers, opposing counsel identities, and financial figures prior to external LLM analysis constitutes demonstrable, state-of-the-art technical precautions that preserve work-product confidentiality.

How should security teams answer SIG Lite, CSA CAIQ, and VSAQ vendor risk questionnaires for a ZTDS appliance? +

For ZTDS-certified local appliances, subprocessor, cloud data residency, and data-at-rest encryption questions are answered as "Not Applicable — Zero Data Egress Architecture (Client-Side Volatile RAM Only)." Cryptographic verification is evidenced via offline Ed25519 signature validation and local SHA-256 compliance receipts generated by npx ztds-audit.
